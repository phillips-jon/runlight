Runlight counts who visits your site, where they came from, and what they did next, from inside your own app, with every number kept in your own database.

It is a library, not a service: a package, one route, and a script under 2 KB. No cookies, nothing personal stored, and no account to make. MIT licensed.

npm install @runlight/sdk better-sqlite3

pnpm add @runlight/sdk better-sqlite3

yarn add @runlight/sdk better-sqlite3

bun add @runlight/sdk better-sqlite3

Paste it into any coding agent to set Runlight up. Also at /prompt.txt.

Dashboard

One page, with everything on it.

It mounts inside your app at /runlight. The first line says how many people came and how that compares; six numbers sit above a chart, and below them the pages, sources, places, devices, campaigns, and AI agents, each one a click away from filtering everything else.

The dashboard as the package serves it, for a month of a demo blog.

Install

Three files, in the app you already have.

One file says where the numbers live, one route serves the script, takes the visits, and shows the dashboard, and one tag counts every page. The script posts to your own domain, so ad blockers have nothing to block.

lib/runlight.ts

import { runlight } from "@runlight/sdk";
import { sqlite } from "@runlight/sdk/sqlite";   // or postgres

export const rl = runlight({
  store: sqlite({ path: "./data/runlight.db" }),
  site: { hostnames: ["example.com"], timezone: "Europe/London" },
});

app/runlight/[[...path]]/route.ts

import { rl } from "@/lib/runlight";

export const { GET, POST, PUT, PATCH, DELETE, OPTIONS } = rl.routes();

app/layout.tsx

<script defer src="/runlight/s.js"></script>

That is Next.js. Express, Hono, SvelteKit, Astro, Remix, and anything else that handles a web Request work the same way: every framework. Set RUNLIGHT_TOKEN and open /runlight to sign in.

Privacy

It counts people without knowing who they are.

Each day Runlight makes a new random salt. A visitor is a hash of that salt, your site, their IP address, and their browser’s user agent, cut to 64 bits. The address and the user agent are never written down, and salts older than yesterday are deleted, so after a day nobody, you included, can tell whether two visits came from the same person. No cookies, nothing in local storage, nothing to put in a banner.

Sources

It knows when a visit came from ChatGPT.

Referrers become sources you recognise, and sources fall into channels: search, social, email, campaigns, and AI, for people who clicked through from ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and the rest. Newsletter click trackers and webmail count as email, not as a referral from some redirect host.

AI agents that fetch your pages to answer a question never run JavaScript, so no tracker sees them. One line of middleware does: rl.observe(request).

Live

What is happening right now, and never who.

Click “here now” for the last half hour minute by minute, the pages people are on, where they came from, and a running line of what they did. It refreshes every ten seconds.

Goals

Conversions, and what they were worth.

A goal is an event you send, a page someone reaches (/thanks* works), or a click on something. Clicks need no code: press “Pick on my site”, click the button on your own page, and Runlight counts it from then on. Give a goal a fixed value or read the amount from the event, and the box shows revenue by goal, source, and page.

Goals are worked out when you read them, so one you add today counts last month too.

Links

Short links on your own domain, counted like visits.

Make t.example.com/sale from the dashboard and every click is counted with its source, country, device, and campaign. Thousands of links are fine. Bring yours over from Umami, Dub, Bitly, Short.io, Rebrandly, or a CSV, with their click history where the service shares it.

Reports

A weekly email that reads like the dashboard.

Pick who gets a weekly or monthly summary, each in their own language. It goes out through the email service you already use, from your own address, with a one-click unsubscribe. Keys are stored encrypted and never shown again.

The dashboard and the emails in English, French, Spanish, German, and Portuguese.
Sites

Stores

API
Everything on the dashboard as JSON at /runlight/api.

Why

Because analytics should sit next to the rest of your data.

I ran my own fork of Umami to get short links on my own domains, and every update meant merging my changes again. Runlight is what I wanted instead: analytics that ships inside the app it measures, keeps its numbers beside everything else I own, and is honest about what it knows.

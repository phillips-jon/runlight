6,920 people visited in the last 30 days, 37% more than the 30 days before.

That is the first line of a Runlight dashboard. Runlight is open source web analytics that installs into your app like any other package. It keeps its numbers in your database, shows them on your own domain, and never sets a cookie.

npm install @runlight/sdk better-sqlite3

Read the docs

Install

A package, a route, and a script tag.

Runlight is a library, not a service. You add it to an app you already run, mount its routes, and put one small script on your pages. The tracker is under 2 KB, and it posts to your own domain, so ad blockers have nothing to block.

lib/runlight.ts

import { runlight } from "@runlight/sdk";
import { sqlite } from "@runlight/sdk/sqlite";

export const rl = runlight({
  store: sqlite({ path: "./data/runlight.db" }),
  site: { hostnames: ["example.com"] },
});

One file says where the numbers live: SQLite, or Postgres with the same API.

app/runlight/[[...path]]/route.ts

import { rl } from "@/lib/runlight";

export const {
  GET, POST, PUT, PATCH, DELETE, OPTIONS,
} = rl.routes();

One route serves the script, takes the visits, and shows the dashboard.

app/layout.tsx

<script defer src="/runlight/s.js"></script>

One tag counts every page. Set RUNLIGHT_TOKEN and open /runlight.

That is Next.js. Express and plain Node, Hono, SvelteKit, Astro, Remix, and anything else that handles a web Request work the same way. See every framework.

Privacy

Counts people without knowing who they are.

Each day Runlight makes a new random salt. A visitor is a hash of that salt, your site, their IP address, and their browser’s user agent, cut to 64 bits. The IP and the user agent are never written down, and salts older than yesterday are deleted, so after a day nobody, you included, can tell whether two visits came from the same person.

No cookies, nothing in local storage, and no fingerprinting beyond that one daily hash. That is why there is nothing to put in a cookie banner.

Sources

Knows when the visit came from ChatGPT.

Referrers become sources you recognise, and sources fall into channels: search, social, email, campaigns, and AI, which counts people who clicked through from ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and the rest. Newsletter click trackers and webmail count as email, not as a referral from some redirect host.

AI agents that read your pages to answer someone’s question never run JavaScript, so no tracker sees them. Runlight does, from one line of middleware: rl.observe(request).

Live

What is happening right now.

Click “here now” and Runlight shows the last half hour minute by minute, the pages people are on, where they came from, and a running line of what they did. It says what happened and where, never who.

Goals

Conversions, and what they were worth.

A goal is an event you send, a page someone reaches (/thanks* works), or a click on something. For clicks there is no code at all: press “Pick on my site”, click the button on your own page, and Runlight counts it from then on. Give a goal a fixed value or read the amount from the event, and the box shows revenue by goal, source, and page.

Goals are worked out when you read them, not when visits come in, so a goal you add today counts last month too.

Links

Short links on your own domain.

Make t.example.com/sale from the dashboard and every click is counted like a visit: source, country, device, and campaign. Thousands of links are fine. Bring yours over from Umami, Dub, Bitly, Short.io, Rebrandly, or a CSV, with their click history where the service shares it.

Reports

A weekly email that reads like the dashboard.

Pick who gets a weekly or monthly summary, in their own language. Runlight sends it through the email service you already use, from your own address, with a one-click unsubscribe. Keys are stored encrypted and never shown again.

Also

The rest of what you would expect.

Share a dashboard
A private link anyone can open, read only, for one site. Delete it and it stops working.
Compare
Against the period before, the same days last year, or any dates you pick.
Filter anything
Page, source, campaign, country, city, device, browser, and more, combined.
Five languages
The dashboard and the emails in English, French, Spanish, German, and Portuguese.
Many sites
One install can count several sites, told apart by hostname.
An API
Everything on the dashboard is JSON at /runlight/api, for your own scripts.

Why

I ran my own fork of Umami so I could have short links on my own domains, and every update meant merging my changes again. Runlight is what I wanted instead: analytics that ships inside the app it measures, keeps its data next to the rest of mine, and is honest about what it knows.
